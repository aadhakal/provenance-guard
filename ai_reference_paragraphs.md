Tree rings offer scientists a detailed record of past climate conditions. Each year, a tree adds a new layer of growth, and the width of that layer reflects the conditions during the growing season. Wide rings typically indicate ample rainfall and favorable temperatures, while narrow rings suggest drought or other stress. By comparing samples from many trees across a region, researchers can reconstruct climate patterns stretching back hundreds or even thousands of years.

Urban heat islands form when cities replace natural land cover with pavement, buildings, and other surfaces that absorb and retain heat. As a result, urban areas can be several degrees warmer than surrounding rural areas, particularly at night. These higher temperatures increase energy demand for cooling, worsen air quality, and pose health risks for vulnerable residents. Strategies such as planting trees, installing green roofs, and using reflective materials can help reduce these effects.
